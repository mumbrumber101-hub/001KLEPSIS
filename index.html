Annals of Klepsis

Introduction

Annals of Klepsis (1983) takes place on Klepsis, the Thief Planet, the most disreputable of three Trader Planets orbiting the Beta Sun in a seventeen-world, four-sun Particular Universe. Long John Tong Tyrone, a one-legged historian, narrates; he arrives on Klepsis to find and record the history of a world that has none.

Thirteen cantos contain genealogies, tall tales, creation myths, scientific symposia, kangaroo courts, and a Doomsday Equation that threatens to collapse the entire humanly inhabited universe. The action runs from Long John’s arrival one afternoon to the morning after his second night on Klepsis. It encompasses a slave sale, a night in the treasure caves, conversations with Christopher Brannagan’s Ghost, a voyage into Brannagan’s projected brain, a scientific congress, Angela’s seizure of power, a twin-switch execution, a legislative banquet, and a climactic race against apocalypse.

Klepsis is said to have no history; literacy is discouraged, and clocks have never caught on. Its government is a “Covenanted Piracy,” its ocean is freshwater and shouts “My Name is Adventure,” and its grapes cause hallucinations. Its people go barefoot with painted feet, or wear wooden legs striped like barber poles. Its subjects are reality and hallucination, history and myth, the claims of ghosts on the living, the mathematics of eschatology, and a sacrificial love whose effect the ending leaves unresolved.

The Thirteen Cantos

I All the Peg-legged Irishmen, or Salt for the Ocean ▶

Opens with a formal “Remember these things” prologue listing the essential persons and places, then narrates the arrival of four visitors on the flight from Apateon: Long John Tong Tyrone (historian), Gold Coast O’Mally (treasure hunter), Conchita O’Brian (coded technology), and Terpsichore Callagy (art). A debtor’s head explodes on the flight when his loan expires and the sliver in his skull detonates. As the ship approaches Klepsis Third Port, they make the ritual offering of salt to the freshwater ocean. After landing, they encounter the planet’s painted-feet pirate culture and the luscious grapes at customs, and witness seven felons play sudden-death poker under the gallows. The winner, an unnamed stranger who turns out to be a prince, buys the largest boat at the dock, hires a crew of twelve, and the company sails out onto the ocean.

II To Ravel-Brannagan Castle ▶

The voyage to Ravel-Brannagan Castle aboard The Dina O’Grogan. Long John observes the freshwater ocean, the Ninety-Nine Continents, the whales mistaken for dolphins, and the flying fish shot with bow-and-arrow. Jerome Whitewater, an “unordinary seaman,” becomes his informant, predicting bloodshed before the second moon rises. They pass cork islands; a young man gives Long John a crumbling newspaper printed by the itinerant Ink-Stained Wretch, containing a slave-sale advertisement for Ravel-Brannagan Castle that night. The shipowner is identified as Prince Franco the Outcast. He says that one twin in three out of four sets of male twins on Klepsis can “go vague” and discourses on Brannagan’s bones, whose coffin shows two intact legs despite the peg-leg legend. The castle comes into view with its six watchtowers. At the dock, a sedated right whale waits to be the gala’s main dish. Franco gives his full titles, goes vague on his cot, and disappears from his clothes. Princess Angela Gilmartin Ravel comes aboard, calls Franco “my secret love,” and plots Prince Henry’s overthrow.

III Oh, Hospitality Most Strong! ▶

The company arrives at the castle, where Prince Henry threatens to execute all sixteen as entertainment. Conchita invokes the Hospitality of Klepsis. Henry locks them in the Whispering Room, a strong room with one-way amplifiers through which they overhear the castle, including a Henry-Ambassador dialogue in which Henry declares “the ultimate freedom is the freedom to own slaves.” Brannagan’s Ghost speaks for the first time: “I am a voice crying in my monument.” Angela opens the door and releases them to fine rooms, each with a money-font. The Castle Library holds exactly four books. One hundred steam calliopes and the smell of 150 tons of whole-roasted whale draw the company outside to the gala and slave sale. Sparaticus bids for his enslaved brother. Long John bids for a slave girl with the “Thorn” sign on her collar. The insurance-advertisement man is dropped from twenty thousand meters and explodes on impact. Spectators hear his approach despite his supersonic fall, the Whittlesby Phenomenon. Long John is hung by his thumbs and whipped after giving Sparaticus the money backing his bid; Angela intervenes, slipping money into his pocket and bidding on his behalf. Angela identifies the slave girl as Tharrala, whose name means “Courage,” and reveals the orange-yellow-red roots of her own hair.

IV The Slaves at the Sale ▶

Opens with the “Klepsis Slave-Sale Song” epigraph. Long John is still hung by his thumbs. Prince Franco, invisible, feeds him whale fat and eases his bondage. The rival bidder for Thorn is found dead, apparently killed by the invisible Franco. Long John takes possession of Thorn, who immediately takes possession of him. She leads him to the giant wine tun in the Castle cellars; he swims in the healing wine, and his whip wounds and broken bones heal. From O’Grogan’s Mountain they observe the Castle’s six watchtowers and their ghosts, the taboo En-Arche Bell Tower (higher than all others, its bell never rung), and five intelligent bears from Astrobe watching through Franco’s telescope. After Baron O’Tolliver’s executions, Prince Henry’s begin. His victims include masked substitutes for members of the company and a girl crushed by elephants in place of Terpsichore. In Brannagan’s walk-in tomb, a Green Robe performs a charade that turns out to be Long John and Thorn’s marriage. Brannagan gives Long John the family pedigree. On leaving, they hear digging in the nearby thickets. Later, Franco arms Long John’s double at the execution site, and a battle erupts. Long John and Thorn flee into the whale.

V Tales of Tarshish ▶

Inside the theatre-in-the-whale are about a hundred persons, two-thirds of them children, lit by whale-oil lamps and devilfish torches. A blind Tarshish storyteller, wearing an Eumolpe Pierce-Brain Light-Harvester mask, performs his tales: the tumbleweed-thistles that carried life between planets before spaceships existed; the unborn universe, in which nothing yet exists and all other things will become imaginings in the mind of whatever wakes first. Thorn confirms the jumping-ships story is literally true: her pirate ancestors did it. Six flute players perform an intermission including “The World Begins Too Soon.” Fifteen more story titles are listed, including “The Stubborn Girl Who Was Crushed to Death by Elephants,” a story about events from the previous canto, already being told. Outside, the David Watchtower burns; the ghost of David Ravel, Thorn’s great-grandfather, screams in agony. The storyteller wakes to insist that ghosts feel “pristine pain undiluted by the body.” The shooting dies down (ammunition runs out on the planet Thorn likens to “a banana republic, without the bananas”). Gold Coast O’Mally breaks up through the whale floor from the treasure caverns below. The children call him a “Tellurian giant.” Long John and Thorn follow him down.

VI Treasure Caves of Klepsis ▶

Opens with the full text of the Brannagan-O’Grogan pedigree: six acknowledged generations, from Christopher Brannagan through the eleven pirates of the third generation to the present twins, followed by Thorn’s disputed claim to a seventh. The company explores the treasure galleries beneath the castle, where seven companions are named as dead. Gold Coast’s chests overflow with gold and gemstones that shine by their own light in sufficient mass. Thorn decodes the signature “T.E.H.C.O.P.O.K.”: The Eleven High Covenanted O’Grogan Pirates of Klepsis. Eleven side passages are guarded by mechanical Deviced Dogs made by an artisan called “Prester John.” Thorn says these collector’s items could hardly be bought for a billion thalers each. Bartolomo identifies their armaments. Kate Blithespirit is drawn to Bluebeard’s passage; Thorn retells the Bluebeard tale. The cavern provisions include Cueva Rica Rum (“still alive and rampant”) and Mock-Madeira Wine. A tax collector, thought to be a wax figure, wakes. Appointed by Rose Lunaria more than a century earlier, he collects the treasure tithe and orders nard every nine years to mask Bluebeard’s stench. The Handbook of the Prophecies of the Family lists Thorn “with a red asterisk” and Long John as “Duke Tyrone the Historian.” The soggy man appears, healed after fifty years in the wine vat, and summons them: Brannagan needs “a combination wizard-philosopher-prophet-politician,” the “most distinguished historian to be found.” “Fake it, man, fake it.”

VII Conversations in a Walk-in Tomb ▶

This canto is the philosophical center of the novel. In the walk-in tomb, Brannagan’s Ghost opens: “Ghosts wear out, you know.” Seneschal Fidelis is named for the first time: the soggy man healed in the wine vat after fifty years. Brannagan reports that another historian arrived and is now buried in the potter’s field: “he did seem more distinguished than you.” Brannagan shows Long John the coffin X-ray: two intact legs in the body, though the ghost has a wooden leg. Only one monocle shared between ghost and corpse. Fidelis argues Brannagan may be a forgotten twin because he has weight, which Fidelis believes true ghosts lack. Brannagan puts his own weight at about one pound. Brannagan’s central question: “I want to find out what is reality and what is not reality.” He lays out three complicating factors: (a) hallucinatory plants, with the “My God What Grapes!” offered as a countermove to make visitors hallucinate the same as natives; (b) phantoms he summoned to populate the planet who refused to leave; (c) himself: “While I still stir and walk and talk, even in my ghost form, then Klepsis is kept in its time of legend and prehistory.” The ninety-percent rule. A Banner Bird flies through the ghost, through Fidelis, and through Long John. Brannagan calls it the only sane creature on Klepsis. He smokes Bandicoot cigars and blows smoke-caricatures of each visitor; Thorn’s shows real malevolence, Long John’s shows weakness. He claims extraordinary powers of evocation and says he once tried to evoke God the Father, who sent an angel: “Be quiet. You are an exasperation.” He announces two projects: reconvene the Commission in Lunacy and voyage through his own brain. Januarius O’Grogan appears at the chapter’s close.

VIII A Commission in Lunacy ▶

Long John identifies the newcomer as Januarius O’Grogan, dead about a hundred fifty years but appearing about thirty. The Commission in Lunacy reconvenes in the walk-in tomb, the same cave where the original was held, under the Sign of the Kangaroo, the emblem of the Royal Kangaroos Liars Association. O’Grogan explains the dragon gold: “The old Klepsis dragons (they were of the species Draco Rufus) mined and minted it here for many thousands of years, apparently,” then claims to be “a liar pure and simple,” Liar of the Year thrice. Long John examines about a hundred coins from the caverns: fifty-six bear dragon faces. Terpsichore has found art “above my fondest imaginings”; Bartolomo and Sebastian saw a dragon cub. A nondescript ghost reads the verifax of Brannagan’s mother. The front says single birth; the illegal reverse side reveals the apprentice doctor reported a possible second birth “gone vague”: the forgotten twin, named Chrysanthus Bygosh Brannagan, who at three weeks could walk and talk and at four months was reading engineering books. The text breaks off incomplete. Joshua Thorn’s ghost presents a “flaming bill of particulars”: Brannagan is a “bat-brained” madman with a one-legged haunt. Brannagan’s defense: people are “mere suggestions made out of long and short broken lines,” too sketchy to be real. The dead man in the coffin chuckles, proof of twinning. Brannagan plans a night voyage on The Dina O’Grogan and names the crew. Thorn and Long John exit; Thorn cries “There is one star too many in the sky!”

IX The Introspections of Brannagan, or One Star Too Many in the Sky ▶

The longest canto in the book opens with Thorn explaining the “One Star Too Many” phenomenon: a murder crying to the sky for vengeance. Long John and Thorn dig up the “eminent historian” Titus Livius Morrison-Bryce from potter’s field; he was buried alive via the oil of the dread sunflower poppy. Thorn performs a Dog Latin resurrection ritual. Titus is furious: “Kill a man, or let him live, but do not bury him alive!” Prince Franco leads them through the caverns to The Dina O’Grogan. The crew refuses to sail except under Brannagan or O’Grogan. The ship herself speaks: “Parley birds have already been here and told me.” O’Grogan takes command; Brannagan is chained to the mast by nine strongmen. Quasimodo appears as a young dwarf, but he is the old “sleeper” at the castle. Brannagan sings ballad verses from the mast, including one that openly reveals the secret of planet-jumping: “And if we jump to other world, / We jump by short-tailed human.” A stowaway spy is caught, mind-stripped, and dumped via leaky lifeboat. O’Grogan tells Long John how they half-killed Brannagan by garrote and half the jury vanished, then marooned him for seven years. The ship makes a planetary jump: saltwater ocean, low gravity, green fronds growing on everyone’s bellies and the hull. Bartolomo Portuguese explains jump-botany: “The interstellar dust that some astronomers write about is interstellar botany.” Both historians claim the title Annals of Klepsis for their works. Brannagan is marooned on a scorching iron island of the Dragons’ Asteroid; he breaks his chains and climbs the cliff like a squirrel. A second jump misjumps into what proves to be Brannagan’s projected brain: a kilometers-wide landscape of red and ocher oceans, millions of sad people “like people who have never been awakened.” Doctor Gilmartin climbs two hundred meters, confirms identification, and when the third jump pulls them out, he falls and is killed. Brannagan’s laughter is heard worlds away: “He’d been having a little fun with one of his Projecting Tricks.”

Characters

Long John Tong Tyrone ▶
Narrator · Visitor · Historian

The first-person narrator. A “probably Eurasian” peg-leg, missing his left leg, who arrives on Klepsis to find and record its nonexistent history. He was once a substitute schoolteacher for one year. Brannagan’s Ghost dubs him “Duke Tyrone the Historian” and entrusts him with the task of writing the Annals. He marries Princess Tharrala Thorn in a charade-that-was-not-a-charade performed by the Green Robe in Canto IV. Both he and the rival historian Titus Livius Morrison-Bryce claim the title Annals of Klepsis for their works. Klepsis gives him legend, prophecy, and hallucination in place of the documentary record he came to compile.

Princess Tharrala Thorn ▶
Royal · Love Interest

Daughter of Princess Placidia and Tarquin Thorn, driven into the wilderness for committing “the unspeakable sin,” whose nature is never revealed despite constant speculation. Long John acquires her at the slave sale. She belongs to the Brannagan family, knows the castle’s secret passages, believes she can deactivate the mechanical dogs, and studied middle mathematics, stoics, and dragonry under Flobert Traxley at the Castle School. She repeatedly urges Long John to “look somewhat distinguished, act somewhat distinguished.” She and Long John marry in Canto IV.

Prince Franco the Outcast ▶
Royal · Twin

Twin brother of Prince Henry, First Earl of Klepsis, Grand Duke of Tarshish, Honorary Citizen of multiple worlds, sometime student of Georgetown University and the Collegium Omnium on Analos. Under sentence of death by his brother. He can “go vague,” disappearing from his clothes and becoming invisible. According to Franco, one twin can do this in three out of four sets of male twins born on Klepsis. He wins a sudden-death poker game, buys a ship, and recruits the company. In Canto XI he takes Henry’s place at the execution and is kicked to death by the donkey, dying with the words: “I always cared for you, Angela, but I couldn’t tell you so. The circumstances wouldn’t allow it.”

Prince Henry the Pirate ▶
Royal · Antagonist

Franco’s twin, the illicit ruler of Klepsis, inhabitant of the sixth (and only occupied) watchtower. He looks exactly like Franco but has a different disposition: surly, violent, truculent. He threatens to execute the arrivals, hosts the slave sale, orders the hangings at the castle gates, and commands the torture traps. He cannot go vague (only Franco can). After Franco dies in his place, Henry flees toward the thickets north of O’Grogan’s Mountain.

Princess Angela Gilmartin Ravel / Empress Angela ▶
Royal · Ruler

“The most beautiful woman on Klepsis (it says so on the coins).” Wife of Prince Henry, secretly allied with Franco. About eighteen in Klepsis years (twenty-six in Gaea-Earth years). Jerome Whitewater attributes her youthful glow to “baths in hippopotamus milk.” She declares herself Empress Angela before the execution. After Franco’s sacrificial death and Henry’s flight, she organizes a consulting legislature in Canto XII, enacts laws by voice vote, introduces clocks and regulated time, and manages the Doomsday crisis.

Christopher Brannagan / Brannagan’s Ghost ▶
Ghost · Founder

The founder, discoverer, and inventor of Klepsis, a peg-legged specter more than two hundred years old. A smelting man and ship designer before becoming a space pirate. He inhabits the walk-in tomb, not the Christopher Watchtower (though the tower bears his name). He worries because Klepsis has no history. He smokes Bandicoot hallucinatory cigars, drinks Tarshish Gin Slings, blows smoke-caricatures, evokes other ghosts, reconvenes a two-hundred-year-old Commission in Lunacy, projects a kilometers-wide illusion of his own brain, and in the final canto stops time itself to hold back the En-Arche Bell: “I could always stop time,” he says, “and I have stopped it now, for a very short while.” He insists he had been a peg-leg: “I was damned sure of it. I remember losing my leg.” Yet the coffin X-ray shows two intact legs. He estimates his weight at about one pound; Seneschal Fidelis argues he may be a forgotten twin rather than a ghost.

Andrew “Gold Coast” O’Mally ▶
Visitor · Treasure Hunter

A “clearly black” peg-leg, missing his left leg. He is “into gold and treasure maps that showed the way to buried gold.” He carries more than a hundred treasure maps and his pockets are full of gold coins. He digs from the caverns upward through the whale floor in Canto V, and gives gold freely to the children in the whale who call him a “Tellurian giant.” The glittering heaps draw him “like magnets to my eyes and hands.”

Conchita O’Brian ▶
Visitor

“Plainly Latino,” missing her right leg. She says she is into “coded technology,” though Long John suspects this is “only a code for what she was really into.” She invokes the ancient Brannagan law of hospitality against Prince Henry. In Canto VI she is reported to be among the seven already killed by Henry’s men.

Terpsichore Callagy ▶
Visitor · Artist

A two-legged visitor (“When are you going to have your leg cut off, Terps?”). She is “into art” and comes to study Klepsis’s deliberately bad art, “the worst art anywhere,” collected and purchased at great expense. She discovers art in the treasure caverns. In the final canto she delivers the aesthetic verdict on the apocalypse: “It’s consummate art whether we personally live or die.”

Quasimodo / The Horseshoe Nail ▶
The Third Focus · The Sleeper

A humpbacked dwarf in the Sleeper’s Room, more than two hundred years old, blind and unable to move. The Doomsday Equation identifies him as the third focus of the kinetic three-dimensional ellipse that constitutes the Particular Universe, its “Horseshoe Nail.” He cannot be allowed to awake and he cannot be allowed to die. He says his mind contains every person and most things in the universe in complete detail. But he qualifies: “Are the images in a mirror only images in that mirror, or may they not have primary forms outside of the mirror also?” He claims two other persons share his state. Christopher Brannagan bought him at a slave market two hundred years ago and Quasimodo became the nearest thing Brannagan had to an executive officer; later Brannagan transferred the cosmic burden to him, crediting his “Total compassion, total goodness, total aptitude for all things.” In Canto XIII, the En-Arche Bell begins to toll as he enters the article of death; Brannagan’s Ghost freezes time to hold back the final notes.

The Tarshish Storyteller ▶
Performer · Philosopher

A blind storyteller who performs in the theatre-in-the-whale. He tells tales of tumbleweeds carrying life between planets, of an unborn universe that has not yet begun, and of Bluebeard in the treasure caves. He cocks his ears when people deny that ghosts or machines can feel pain and insists that such things do suffer. In another story, the Tarshish people, who are “unconscious and unborn,” build better spaceships by intuition than any conscious species. He claims to dream everything that happens on Klepsis, up to the point where the slave-mathematician is struck by lightning.

Jerome Whitewater ▶
Seaman · Informant

An “unordinary seaman” who unfurls sails and lands on deck “like a cat—like a cat with one wooden leg.” He is Long John’s primary informant about Klepsis during the voyage to the castle. He predicts bloodshed before the second moon rises, and knows the castle and its people intimately.

Januarius O’Grogan ▶
Ghost · Patriarch

Second-generation ruler of Klepsis, husband of Margaret Brannagan, one of the fountainheads of the “black-haired and olive-skinned strain” in the family. Evoked by Brannagan’s Ghost to testify in the Commission in Lunacy. He explains the dragon origins of the treasure-cave gold. He is “not at all of the flaming orange-yellow-red descent” and speaks in an “intricate voice.”

Aloysius Shiplap ▶
Scientist · Hero

“Aloysius (The Brain Crying in the Wilderness) Shiplap from Gaea-Earth.” A scientist at the Doomsday symposium who exposes the Asteroid Pythagoras as the author of the fraudulent equation. In the final canto, after the unnamed slave-mathematician’s hand is burned to an ember by Doomsday Lightning and the equation paper charred, Aloysius gathers the fragments and reconstructs the solution. Lightning strikes him head-on: “‘Twas life or death for him for a minute there. And then it was life.” His hair catches fire: “I work best when my hair is on fire.”

Malabu Worldwinger ▶
Scientist · Billionaire

An “exuberant young billionaire from World Abounding” who booms “I can move anything” in his “omnipotent voice.” He wants an epitaph better than the one he attributes to Copernicus: “He could have moved them both.” Angela signs permits for him to move Tarshish to a new orbit, requiring four hundred shiploads of heavy equipment. He departs on this mission during the final crisis.

The Asteroid Pythagoras ▶
Nonhuman · Mathematician

A bird, the only one that rumbles. Sibling of the Asteroid Midas, “possibly the richest creature in the universe.” An ostrichlike bird with gigantiornis wings and cheirornin hands. One of the most intelligent entities at the scientific symposium; borderline socially acceptable because she goes after opponents’ eyes with her beak. She fights with Becky Breaksticks over gender parity (“Male pig-bird!”; Oliver Roundhead whispers that the Pythagoras is female). In the final canto, Aloysius Shiplap exposes her as the author of the Doomsday Equation itself, “a masterpiece of advocacy mathematics” designed to end humans but not bird-men. She jabs out three captors’ eyeballs and ascends into the lightning.

Bartolomo Portuguese ▶
Crewman · Weapons Expert

A crewman from The Dina O’Grogan with “the pseudonym of an old Gaea pirate.” Originally from Far Tarshish. He wears his hair in a tarred pigtail and has a tailpiece at the back of his trousers. He is a short-tailed human, one of those who can jump ships between planets. A sixth-generation pirate. He has been in death cells before and “put on a memorable show” at his previous executions. He identifies the mechanical dogs’ armaments. In Canto IX he explains the botany of planet-jumping: “Jump space is fantastically populated—with botany. My God, what botany! The interstellar dust that some astronomers write about is interstellar botany.” His grandfather sailed on The Dina O’Grogan in the great age of piracy.

Seneschal Fidelis ▶
Retainer · Survivor

“The soggy man.” He was whipped and stabbed and believed killed by the servants of Cloud Ravel, then thrown in derision into the vat of healing wine and told to heal himself. The wine healed him after fifty years; he emerges in Canto VI and is named in Canto VII. He argues that Brannagan’s Ghost may be a forgotten twin rather than a true ghost, since he weighs about one pound and “true ghosts do not have weight.” He attends Brannagan’s Ghost in the walk-in tomb and runs errands for him, fetching the Penny Philosopher through the underground passages.

Titus Livius Morrison-Bryce ▶
Visitor · Rival Historian

“The Eminent Historian.” Summoned to Klepsis by Brannagan but buried alive via elaionheliotropionmekon (oil of the dread sunflower poppy). Thorn and Long John unearth and revive him with a Dog Latin ritual in potter’s field in Canto IX. He was the one Thorn intended to marry. Initially furious and crabby (“Kill a man, or let him live, but do not bury him alive!”), he becomes a partisan of Klepsis. He claims the title Annals of Klepsis for his own work, in competition with Long John. He identifies Grand Marshal Golconde as a spy. He approves Fairbridge’s “cliffs of imagination” line, writing it in his pocket notebook.

Fairbridge Exendine ▶
Crewman · Penny Philosopher

“The only ‘penny philosopher’ I ever knew who was worth a farthing.” From the Trader Planet Emporion. He explains the biological jump between planets (“Crossing the Ocean of Hiatus,” “Sailing Through the Bushes”) and shows the green fronds growing from his belly as proof. He speaks the epitaphic line: “Do not ever climb so high on the cliffs of imagination that the fall from the top will kill you.” A member of the ship’s crew, he first appears in Canto II. Seneschal Fidelis fetches him through the underground passages to the walk-in tomb.

Doctor Luke Gilmartin ▶
Scientist · Physician

A living man, not a ghost. Official Doctor of the Realm. In all six generations of Klepsis there has always been a Doctor Gilmartin, and all have looked remarkably alike. He is a grandfather or great-uncle of Princess Angela. First mentioned in Canto VIII; he boards The Dina O’Grogan for the night voyage. He introduces the Doomsday Equation to Long John: “Its revealing is certainly the most important historical event of the century.” He climbs two hundred meters up the cliff of Brannagan’s projected brain, confirms it is Brannagan’s brain from a spot he recognizes, and falls when the jump pulls them out, but survives: “A fall of a millimeter isn’t going to kill me, not if I keep things in proportion.”

Flobert Traxley ▶
Scholar · Dragon Expert

“The Man Who Talks to Dragons.” He holds the Chair of Dragon Studies at the Imperial University of Klepsis, housed in three dim rooms in the Castle attics. Tharrala Thorn studied middle mathematics, stoics, and dragonry under him at the Castle School. He can read the inscriptions on dragon-minted coins. He boards The Dina O’Grogan for the night voyage, where he worries: “If I know my dragons, something very strange and unpleasant is going to happen when the dragons eat Brannagan’s Brain.” On the Dragons’ Asteroid: “It will not be safe for the Brannagan to be marooned anywhere on this planet.”

Becky Breaksticks ▶
Scientist · Legislator

From Dahae. At the symposium she demands gender parity (“Why am I the only female here?”), fights with the Asteroid Pythagoras (“Male pig-bird!”), and delivers the canto’s title line: “I asked for the possibility of worms. I certainly did not ask for the actuality of worms.” In Canto XII she executes a triple power grab, seizing the posts of Speaker of the House, Absolute Master of the Treasure Caverns, and Master of the Dungeons. She deals gold to the barbarians as “a merciless dealer.” On the prophetic lightning: “The bon mots are outright plagiarisms, and the rhymes are impure.”

The Green Robe ▶
Clergy · Confessor

A member of the Order of Saint Klingensmith and “a thrice grandnephew” of Brannagan. He presides at the book’s ritual moments: he performs the charade marriage of Long John and Thorn in Canto IV, lights Bandicoot cigars for Brannagan’s Ghost, gives Quasimodo the “sacraments of the living” (his preferred term for last rites), and confesses the prince believed to be Henry, actually Franco, at the donkey’s tail with beeswax in the donkey’s ears. On the marooning: “The Brannagan will know how to kill a banquet-sized dragon. And Quasimodo will know how to make a banquet out of it.”

Hektor Lafcadio ▶
Crewman · Artisan

The “Greek God” from World Abounding (Aphthonia). A crewman on The Dina O’Grogan. He engraves the names of the seven dead companions on blank plaques in the cavern wall, under the heading “Those Who Have Died In Irregular Seas And Skies”; several hundred names are already there. He climbs the main mast and whispers to the ship that she is going on the most dumbfounding voyage ever; the ship answers, “I know it, I know it,” adding, “Parley birds have already been here and told me.”

Themes

History and Prehistory ▶

Long John arrives on Klepsis to be its historian, but the planet is said to have no history. Literacy is discouraged, dates are difficult for many inhabitants to calculate, and clocks have never caught on. When he asks for maps, a girl replies: “Why should you look at a picture of a thing rather than at the thing itself?” The newspaper’s maker can call its date only “today,” though other characters give precise calendar dates. Angela later declares that time and history on Klepsis will begin at dawn, just when the Doomsday Equation threatens to end the wider human universe.

Brannagan’s Ghost commissions an historian to distinguish reality from hallucination before his ghostly existence fades. Yet he praises illiteracy while admitting that he and almost everybody in officialdom are literate. The narrator’s opening injunction, “Remember these things,” gives the annals an oral-epic form. Long John seeks documentary evidence; Klepsis offers legend, prophecy, and hallucination as well. When Angela orders clocks in Canto XII, she presents them as the start of time and history on Klepsis, not as a convenience.

Reality and Hallucination ▶

Klepsis is a planet where hallucination is normal. The grapes cause it. The animals cause it (“Crazy Deer Planet,” “Drunken Bird Planet,” “Dancing Fish Planet”). The ninety-percent rule provides a practical convention: if ninety percent of people see a thing, it is to be regarded as real. Brannagan cannot remember whether this is called the “Pragmatic Sanction” or the “Categorical Imperative.”

The line between real and unreal is not stable anywhere in the book. Ghosts drink gin slings and smoke cigars, and the substances are actually consumed. A Banner Bird flies through ghosts and living people alike. Brannagan projects a kilometers-wide illusion of his brain that the company sails through; then the dockmaster insists they never left dock. The Tarshish storyteller insists the universe has not yet begun. Brannagan introduced the “My God What Grapes!” to synchronize hallucinations across visitors and natives. He summoned phantoms to populate his world and many refused to leave. Quasimodo holds all persons in his mind but qualifies: “Are the images in a mirror only images in that mirror, or may they not have primary forms outside of the mirror also?”

The Doomsday Equation and Eschatology ▶

The Particular Universe is described as a mathematical construct, a kinetic three-dimensional ellipse with three foci, one of which is a person. The Doomsday Equation predicts that when this person dies, the construct will collapse. Brannagan praises its mathematics as “the most elegant mathematics of any equation ever,” but the final canto exposes the equation’s partisan origins and presents a correction that promises to remove its doom.

In the final canto the threat becomes physical: earthquakes split the mountain, the ocean rises to the castle’s fourth story, and the Lightning, personified, mumbles “It’s now or never.” The unnamed slave-mathematician carries the amended equation up the mountain; Doomsday Lightning burns his hand to an ember and renders him mute. Aloysius Shiplap reconstructs the solution from charred fragments while lightning strikes him head-on. The Asteroid Pythagoras, unmasked as the equation’s author, intended it as “advocacy mathematics” to end humans but not bird-men. The narrative ends mid-strike.

Twins and Doubling ▶

Franco and Henry are the primary pair: identical in appearance, opposite in character, one capable of going vague and one not. Other pairs include Sparaticus and his brother, and Brannagan and his possible “forgotten twin” or “what-is-it.” Franco says that three out of four sets of male twins on Klepsis include one who can go vague. He also claims that the vague twin will always outlive the normal twin, an assurance his own sacrifice overturns.

The doubling extends to the planet itself: Tarshish and Klepsis are two halves of the same world, at once “a hellhole and a treasure place.” Franco’s substitution for Henry at the execution is the moral climax: the good twin dies for the bad twin. The chapter title is “Greater Love Has No Man.”

Ghosts and Secondary Life ▶

Klepsis is crowded with ghosts. Five of the six watchtowers are inhabited by them. The Sleeper’s Room has death masks built into its walls. Brannagan’s Ghost dominates: he smokes, drinks, evokes other ghosts, conducts legal proceedings, projects a kilometers-wide illusion of his own brain, and in the final canto stops time itself to hold back the En-Arche Bell. But he is wearing out: “Ghosts wear out, you know.” He is trying to leave something of himself before he fades.

The Tarshish storyteller insists that ghosts can feel “pristine pain undiluted by the body,” contradicting Thorn’s dismissal of the screaming Ghost of David Ravel. Seneschal Fidelis emerges from healing wine after fifty years of recovery from supposedly fatal assaults. The disagreement over ghostly suffering is partly a question about bodily reality and partly a question about who is owed compassion.

Piracy, Hospitality, and Governance ▶

Klepsis’s government is a “Covenanted Piracy.” Prince Henry invokes this category to evade the prohibitions on slavery that apply to the recognized forms of government. The pirate ethos produces both violence (Prince Henry’s casual executions, the slave sale, the torture traps) and generosity (the font of money in every guest room, the law of hospitality that Conchita invokes).

Angela’s legislative session in Canto XII replaces the Covenanted Piracy with an imperial government and a consulting legislature. She arrives on a hippopotamus, then dismounts to address the gathering. By voice vote, with Becky Breaksticks as scribe, the assembly makes the treasure communal property, restricts its removal, substitutes barter and flintstone coins for gold currency, and resolves to shoot the tails off anyone Prince Henry brings against them. Angela also declares that time and history will begin at dawn.

Sacrifice and Substitution ▶

Franco, the good twin, takes the place of Henry, the bad twin, at the execution. He is kicked to death by a donkey while speaking words of love. The chapter title, “Greater Love Has No Man,” quotes John 15:13 directly. The pattern is Christological, but the death itself is grotesque: a kicking by a donkey, followed by the rotten laughter of the surviving twin as he runs.

The unnamed slave-mathematician runs the amended equation up the mountain; lightning burns his hand to an ember and renders him mute. Aloysius Shiplap then gathers the charred fragments and reconstructs the solution while lightning sets his hair on fire. Their injuries are the cost of an effort to save the universe. The two climactic sacrifices run in parallel: Franco’s is personal and familial, the slave’s and Aloysius’s are cosmic and mathematical. Aloysius announces success, but the final lightning strike leaves its outcome unnarrated.

Timeline

The action runs from the afternoon of Long John’s arrival to the second following dawn. The sequence below follows relative markers such as sun positions, moonrises, midnight bells, and the recurring “white dawn.” Calendar dates already exist: Long John gives the twenty-eighth of Arpad in Canto IV, and Thorn gives the twenty-ninth in Canto VI. Clocks and watches have also reached Klepsis, although they have not caught on. In Canto XII, Angela declares that history will begin at the next dawn and orders clocks offered for sale.

Canto I

Long John Tong Tyrone, Gold Coast O’Mally, Conchita O’Brian, and Terpsichore Callagy arrive on the flight from Apateon. A debtor’s head explodes via skull-sliver. Day One, Evening
Canto III

Arrival at the castle. Prince Henry threatens execution of all sixteen; Conchita invokes the Hospitality of Klepsis. The company is locked in the Whispering Room. Sparaticus bids for his brother, and Long John bids for the slave girl with the “Thorn” sign. Day One, Night
Canto IV

Canto XI

Cosmology & Geography

The Particular Universe

A kinetic three-dimensional ellipse with three foci, consisting of four suns and seventeen habitable worlds. The four suns are Sol (around which orbits Gaea-Earth), Proxima, Alpha, and Beta. The Trader Planets, Emporion, Apateon, and Klepsis, orbit Beta Sun. According to the Doomsday Equation, the system is unstable: its third focus is simultaneously a point and a person (the Horseshoe Nail), whose death would collapse the construct. Other notable worlds include Astrobe, Camiroi, Analos, and World Abounding.

Klepsis

The Thief Planet, the Pirate Planet. Three-quarters ocean in area, with the only freshwater ocean known among the seventeen habitable worlds. The land consists of the Ninety-Nine Continents, which are “really tentacled islands, of long extent down their peninsulas, but not of great area.” The planet is always lit: when Beta Sun sets, the more distant Proxima or Alpha suns keep the night at “cloudy day” level. There are no towns, no trains, hardly any planes. People travel by ship. Bare feet are painted in primary colors for a Klepsis farthing. The “My God What Grapes!” grapes cause hallucination. According to Franco, three out of four sets of male twins include one who can “go vague.”

Ravel-Brannagan Castle

The seat of power on Klepsis. Built mostly of peat-bricks (which burn intensely), with some coursed stone and almost no wood. Distinguishable by its six very tall watchtowers, named for the six successive rulers: the Christopher, Januarius, Juda, David, Cloud, and Henry Watchtowers. Five are inhabited by the ghosts of their namemen. The sixth (Henry) is inhabited by the living Prince Henry. Even higher than the watchtowers stands the En-Arche Bell Tower, surrounded by taboo. The castle contains the Whispering Room (with one-way amplifiers), the Sleeper’s Room (where Quasimodo is kept and death masks line the walls), guest rooms with fonts of money, and passages leading down to the treasure caverns.

The Treasure Caverns

A vast underground complex beneath the castle, accessible through old shafts. Thousands of chests and barrels hold gold coins, gemstones, and artifacts. In a sample of roughly a hundred coins, Long John finds fifty-six bearing dragon faces. Januarius O’Grogan claims that the Draco Rufus mined and minted the old dragon gold “for many thousands of years,” though he also calls himself a liar. The gold and gems shine by their own light in sufficient mass. Eleven side passages branch from the main gallery, each guarded by a Deviced Dog. These mechanical guardians, made by an artisan called “Prester John,” carry homing rockets, incinerating ray nodules, Beauclaire Blasters, and nerve gas; a billion thalers would scarcely buy one. The nard ordered every nine years masks the stench of Bluebeard (Levi O’Grogan-Brannagan), whose rotting body lies in one of the side passages. A tax collector appointed by Rose Lunaria sits at his post after more than a century. Provisions include Cueva Rica Rum (“still alive and rampant”), Mock-Madeira Wine (“lively and sassy”), and the flesh of Thousand-Year Turtles.

Tarshish

A supposedly separate, hidden planet whose location is a guarded secret. Sidonia of Tarshish says that passengers leaving by spacecraft are blindfolded, deafened, brain-pierced, and sedated, and that an anti-scientific clique has always ruled the place. The Tarshish storyteller describes its people as “unconscious and unborn,” yet able to build better spaceships than the people of any other planet. In the final canto comes the revelation: Tarshish and Klepsis are “the opposite parts of the same planet,” two halves of one planetary body. The saying “If Tarshish moves, can Klepsis long stand still?” turns out to be literally true.

The Ocean

Freshwater and saltless, running between the Ninety-Nine Continents in channels broader than they first appear: what seem to be dolphins turn out to be right whales. The ocean “seemed to shout, ‘My name is adventure.’” Visitors are expected to bring offerings of salt to pour into it. The flying fish are brighter-colored than Gaea-Earth’s, toothed, and meaty. The ocean is wine-colored.

Genealogy

The Brannagan-O’Grogan dynasty as dictated by Brannagan’s Ghost in Canto VI. Six generations of rulers, pirates, and outcasts, with Tharrala Thorn claiming a place in the seventh. The six watchtowers are named for the six successive rulers (marked with ◆).

◆ Christopher Brannagan: Founder of Klepsis. Peg-legged pirate, smelting man, stage magician. Married Margaret Summertime (née Thyme, widow of John Summers; she compounded her surname from Summers + Thyme). His apparent ghost inhabits the walk-in tomb; whether it is a ghost or a surviving twin is disputed. Disputed: whether he fathered Margaret or whether John Summers did. “I say that she was my daughter.”

Margaret Brannagan married ◆ Januarius O’Grogan (from Tarshish)

◆ Juda O’Grogan-Brannagan: “A gentle man.” Married Rose Lunaria. His haunt sings “Rosa, Rosa, Rosa” in his watchtower.

Ruben O’Grogan-Brannagan: Pirate who ravaged World Abounding nine times.

Levi O’Grogan-Brannagan: Bluebeard the Pirate. Thirteen wives, more than a hundred children. His rotting body is in the treasure caverns.

Simeon O’Grogan-Brannagan: Buried 10,000 large chests of gold and gemstones near the castle.

Dan O’Grogan-Brannagan: “A cruel and crafty pirate.”

Nefthal O’Grogan-Brannagan: “He put ten thousand persons to the sword.”

Gad O’Grogan-Brannagan: “The Gold of Gad” is proverbial.

Aser O’Grogan-Brannagan: Gave up piracy and became an Ocean Marshal.

Issachar O’Grogan-Brannagan: Killed while still a youth.

Zabulon O’Grogan-Brannagan: Also killed while a youth, “but he did a lot of killing of his own first.”

Dina O’Grogan-Brannagan: Pirate; the ship The Dina O’Grogan is named for her. Her crews “were more savage than the crews of any of the other tall pirate ships.”

All sons except Juda and Joseph were pirates, as was Dina. Their eleven tall ships were the terror of Klepsis and raided the seas of almost every other planet. This is the third generation.

Glossary

A
Alex Braveheart Cantos X, XIII
Scientist from Camiroi. Participates in the Doomsday Equation symposium. During the final crisis, he explains that Tarshish and Klepsis are opposite aspects of the same planet.
Aloysius Shiplap Cantos X, XII, XIII
“Aloysius (The Brain Crying in the Wilderness) Shiplap from Gaea-Earth.” A scientist at the Doomsday symposium. In Canto XIII he identifies the Asteroid Pythagoras as the equation’s author. After the unnamed slave-mathematician’s hand is burned, Aloysius gathers the charred equation fragments and reconstructs the solution. Lightning strikes him head-on; his hair catches fire: “I work best when my hair is on fire.”
Analos Canto I
One of the seventeen habitable worlds. Prince Franco is an Honorary Citizen and sometime student of the Collegium Omnium on Analos.
Angela Gilmartin Ravel Cantos II–XIII
Princess Angela, later Empress Angela. “The most beautiful woman on Klepsis (it says so on the coins).” Wife of Prince Henry, secretly allied with Franco. After Franco’s death and Henry’s flight, she declares herself Empress and establishes a consulting legislature.
Angela Bell Tower Canto XI
A new bell tower under construction at Ravel-Brannagan Castle, signaling Angela’s ascendancy. Workmen begin building it as Prince Henry’s power collapses.
Apateon Cantos I, VI
One of the three Trader Planets orbiting Beta Sun. “Apateon has no ethics.” The flight from Apateon to Klepsis opens the story.
Asteroid Midas Canto X
Sibling of the Asteroid Pythagoras. “Possibly the richest creature in the universe, who has a pinion on every planet.”
Asteroid Pythagoras Cantos X, XIII
A bird described as “the only bird that rumbles.” A nonhuman mathematician at the scientific symposium. She fights with Becky Breaksticks over gender parity. In the final canto she strikes her three captors’ eyes with her beak and ascends into the lightning to observe the test of her equation.
Astrobe Cantos I, IV, IX–X, XII
One of the seventeen worlds. Prince Franco is an Honorary Citizen. Brannagan was a stage magician there in his youth. The five bears from Astrobe space-jumped to Klepsis.
B
Bancroft Romal Cantos XI–XIII
Scientist who addresses Brannagan’s Ghost as “old tainted patriarch” in his “friendly way.” He represents Gaea-Earth in the proposal to destroy one planet to stabilize the universe.
Bandicoot cheroots / Bandicoot hallucinatory cigars Cantos II, VII
Hallucinatory cigars smoked by Brannagan’s Ghost. The Green Robe lights them for him. He uses the smoke to blow sculptured caricatures of each visitor.
Banner Bird Canto VII
A small, brilliantly colored bird that flies through both ghosts and living people. It flies through Brannagan’s Ghost (acceptable), through Seneschal Fidelis (almost acceptable), and then through Long John himself: “That was not all right, not all right at all.” The species “worries” Brannagan.
Bartolomo Portuguese Cantos II–VI, VIII, IX, XIII
Crewman from The Dina O’Grogan with “the pseudonym of an old Gaea pirate.” Originally from Far Tarshish. A short-tailed human and sixth-generation pirate. In Canto II he tells how Brannagan brought seven right whales from Gaea in bottle-nosed Folly Ships. He identifies the mechanical dogs’ weaponry in Canto VI. In Canto IX he explains planet-jumping botany: “The interstellar dust that some astronomers write about is interstellar botany.”
Beauclaire Blasters Canto VI
One of the weapon systems built into the mechanical guardian dogs of the treasure caverns. Identified by Bartolomo Portuguese.
Becky Breaksticks Cantos X, XII, XIII
Scientist and scribe. She demands gender parity at the symposium: “Why am I the only female here?” She fights with the Asteroid Pythagoras. In Canto XII she is Angela’s legislative scribe, writing down the new laws.
Beta Sun Cantos I, II
The nearest of the four suns to the Trader Planets. Klepsis, Apateon, and Emporion orbit it. The “inelegant planets” cluster here.
Brannagan’s Ghost Cantos I–XIII
See Christopher Brannagan. The peg-legged specter of the founder of Klepsis. Inhabits a walk-in tomb near the castle. Smokes Bandicoot hallucinatory cigars, evokes other ghosts, reconvenes the Commission in Lunacy, projects a kilometers-wide illusion of his brain, and in the final canto stops time itself to hold back the En-Arche Bell. He is wearing out: “Ghosts wear out, you know.”
Brigid Hearn Canto VI
Wife of Cloud Ravel-Brannagan. Mother of Princess Placidia and the twins Prince Henry and Prince Franco.
C
Camiroi Cantos I, VII, X
One of the seventeen worlds, culturally superior. Brannagan insists “a genuine Klepsis is better than an imitation Camiroi.” Klepsis Clubs are fashionable on Camiroi among young people. Kate Blithespirit is from Camiroi.
Castle School Cantos VIII, XI
Located in “three dim and dusty rooms up in the attics of Ravel-Brannagan Castle.” Flobert Traxley chairs dragon studies there. Tharrala Thorn studied middle mathematics and stoics and dragonry from him. Student body may be as few as two or three.
Christopher Brannagan Cantos I–XIII
The founder, discoverer, and inventor of Klepsis. A peg-legged man who was a smelting man, ship designer, and stage magician before becoming a space pirate. He was deprived of his office for killing Joshua Thorn during the Commission in Lunacy and was marooned on the most desolate asteroid in the universe for seven years. He now appears as a ghost, although the possibility that he is a forgotten twin complicates that identity. His body, viewed in its glass coffin, has two intact legs, contradicting the legend.
Clarence Pinnacle Cantos XI–XII
Scientist from Analos. He represents Analos in the planet-destruction proposal and questions Brannagan about his claim to have held all the persons of the universe in his mind.
Cloud Ravel-Brannagan Canto VI
Fifth ruler of Klepsis. Married Brigid Hearn. Father of Placidia and the twins Henry and Franco. His servants whipped and stabbed Seneschal Fidelis, believing they had killed him.
Commission in Lunacy Canto VIII
A kangaroo court originally convened two hundred years ago to settle questions of Brannagan’s sanity and legitimacy. Reconvened in Canto VIII with evoked ghosts as witnesses. The original proceedings led to Brannagan killing Joshua Thorn and being deprived of power.
Conchita O’Brian Cantos I–VI
“Plainly Latino” peg-leg, missing her right leg. Says she is into “coded technology.” She invokes the ancient Brannagan law of hospitality against Prince Henry. Killed by Henry’s men; one of the seven dead listed in Canto VI.
Covenanted Piracy Cantos I, XII
The form of government on Klepsis for two hundred years, described as “not any of those types” (democracy, oligarchy, tyranny, etc.). Replaced in Canto XII when Angela establishes a consulting legislature.
Cueva Rica Rum Canto VI
Provisions in the treasure caverns. “Still alive and rampant in its glass casks” after centuries of storage. Contrasted with the dead sea biscuits and evaporated sweet water.
D
David Ravel Cantos V–VI
“The Elegant Interloper.” Fourth-generation ruler of Klepsis. His watchtower burns in Canto V; his ghost screams in agony, raising the debate on whether ghosts can feel pain.
Decimus Gormley Canto X
A nonhuman from World Abounding (Aphthonia). At the scientific symposium he unrolls a “completed and corrected” Doomsday Equation; the room falls silent for an hour. His version provides for the planets to survive only as “dragonly inhabited planets,” not humanly inhabited ones.
Dina O’Grogan-Brannagan Cantos VI, XI
Daughter of Margaret and Januarius. A pirate. The ship The Dina O’Grogan is named for her. She had the death-mask traps built by “the finest trap-builder of the worlds.”
Doomsday Equation Epigraph, Cantos X–XIII
The mathematical formulation predicting the collapse of the Particular Universe when its third focus, the Horseshoe Nail, dies. Described as “the most elegant mathematics of any equation ever.” Originated on an asteroid, not a planet. In the final canto, an unnamed slave-mathematician carries the amended equation up the mountain but is struck by Doomsday Lightning; Aloysius Shiplap then reconstructs the solution from the charred fragments.
Doomsday Lightning Cantos III, XIII
First appears in Canto III, killing three persons during the slave sale. In Canto XIII it is personified, striking first at the unnamed slave-mathematician (burning his hand to an ember) and then at Aloysius Shiplap (head-on, setting his hair on fire). “‘It’s now or never,’ the Lightning mumbled.” The narrative ends with the final, frustrated, erratic strike.
Draco Rufus Cantos VI, VIII
The old Klepsis dragons who mined and minted gold “for many thousands of years.” The treasure caves contain dragon-minted coins. Flobert Traxley, “the Man Who Talks to Dragons,” can read the inscriptions.
E
Emporion Canto I
One of the three Trader Planets orbiting Beta Sun. “Emporion has no law.”
En-Arche Bell Tower Cantos II, IV, XIII
Higher than the six regular watchtowers; utterly taboo. “People did not raise up their eyes or their voices” toward it. First described in the watchtower tour of Canto IV. Its bell begins to toll as Quasimodo enters the article of death in Canto XIII; Brannagan’s Ghost stops time to freeze it. Thorn reinterprets the bell: Brannagan named the tower “En-Arche” from the first words of the Septuagint Bible (“In the Beginning”) and built it to ring when the world begins, not when it ends.
Eumolpe device Canto V
A prosthetic sight device. Its prods pierce into the brain and offer “the Pragmatic Equivalent of Sight.” Worn by the blind Tarshish storyteller as a glass-and-metal mask over his eyes.
F
Fairbridge Exendine Cantos VI, XII
Crewman from The Dina O’Grogan. Survives the massacre. Seated at the friends’ table at the banquet in Canto XII.
Flobert Traxley Cantos VIII, X, XI
“The Man Who Talks to Dragons.” Chairs dragon studies at the Castle School. He can read the inscriptions on dragon-minted coins. Tharrala Thorn studied mathematics, stoics, and dragonry under him.
“For want of a nail” Canto X epigraph
Traditional nursery rhyme expanded to cosmic scale. Ends: “For want of the world the cosmos is lost, / And all for the loss of the Horseshoe Nail.” Attributed to “Nescio Unde.” Connects the Horseshoe Nail motif to Quasimodo.
Forgotten-twin phenomenon Cantos II, VII, VIII, XI
A mysterious condition in which a twin is born but then “goes vague” immediately or is somehow forgotten. On Klepsis, three out of four sets of male twins include one who can go vague. The insane twin “is inclined to break all the laws of physics and nature.” The central question of the Commission in Lunacy is whether Brannagan was a twin.
Prince Franco Cantos I–XI
Prince Franco the Outcast. Full name: Prince Franco Ravel-O’Grogan-Brannagan. The good twin who dies for his bad twin brother at the donkey’s tail.
G
Gaea-Earth Throughout
Earth, orbiting Sol. “The only one of the worlds that has only one sun and one moon.” According to the Tarshish storyteller, its people maintain that the universe is 2,200 years old. Long John, Aloysius Shiplap, and others originate here.
“Going vague” Cantos II, VII, XI
An ability associated with certain male twins on Klepsis: becoming invisible, leaving their clothes behind, and later reappearing. Prince Franco can do it; Prince Henry cannot. Franco claims that “The vague twin will always outlive the normal twin.” The trait turns up in one out of thirty thousand male twins on Gaea-Earth but three out of four sets of male twins on Klepsis.
Gold Coast O’Mally Cantos I–VI
Andrew “Gold Coast” O’Mally, a “clearly black” peg-leg with a hundred treasure maps and pockets always full of gold coins. He digs from the treasure caverns upward through the whale floor in Canto V.
Green Robe Cantos IV, VII, IX, XI
A member of the order of Saint Klingensmith, and “a thrice grandnephew” of Brannagan. He performs the charade-marriage of Long John and Thorn in Canto IV, lights Bandicoot cigars for the ghost, and hears the confession of the condemned prince (Franco in Henry’s place) before the execution.
H
Handbook of the Prophecies of the Family Cantos VI–VII
A prophetic book held by the tax collector in the treasure caverns. It contains entries for family members including Tharrala Thorn (“with a red asterisk”) and Long John (“Duke Tyrone the Historian”). Long John is legally entitled to a copy.
Hektor Lafcadio Cantos VI, XII
One of the company. He engraves the names of the seven dead companions on blank plaques in the cavern wall. Several hundred names are already there, under the title “Those Who Have Died In Irregular Seas And Skies.”
Prince Henry Ravel-Brannagan the Pirate Cantos I–XIII
The illicit ruler of Klepsis, Franco’s twin. Inhabits the sixth watchtower. Surly, violent, and truculent. He hosts the slave sale, orders hangings, and hunts investigators in the Sleeper’s Room. He cannot go vague. After Franco dies in his place, he flees north laughing. His head is later brought on a lance by feral people.
Hogson Roadapple Cantos III–VI
“An undistinguished person from Hokey Planet.” One of the crew. Gives money to Sparaticus to bid for his brother. Killed by Prince Henry’s men.
Horseshoe Nail Epigraph, Cantos X–XIII
The code name for Quasimodo, the person who is the third focus of the Particular Universe. From the “For want of a nail” poem: according to the Doomsday Equation, the entire humanly inhabited cosmos depends on this single, ancient, sleeping person.
I
Isadora Ragsley Cantos XI–XII
Scientist from Paravata. She warns Bancroft Romal about Brannagan’s charm: “Watch him, Bancroft, he can charm the birds out of the trees.” She helps investigate the Franco-Henry twin relationship.
Issachar O’Grogan-Brannagan Cantos VI, XI
One of the sons of Margaret and Januarius. His death mask is in the Sleeper’s Room.
J
Januarius O’Grogan Cantos VI, VIII
Second-generation ruler of Klepsis, from Tarshish. Married Margaret Brannagan. Father of the eleven pirates. His complexion is “not at all of the flaming orange-yellow-red descent”; he is one of the fountainheads of the black-haired, olive-skinned strain. Evoked by Brannagan’s Ghost in the Commission in Lunacy.
Jerome Whitewater Cantos II–III, XII
“Unordinary seaman” on The Dina O’Grogan. Lands on deck “like a cat—like a cat with one wooden leg.” Long John’s primary informant about Klepsis during the voyage. Predicts bloodshed before the second moon rises.
Joseph O’Grogan Canto VI
Januarius’s son by a previous marriage and Margaret’s adopted son. “The eldest of my tall sons, though from his boyish look you’d take him for the youngest.” He and Juda “are the only ones who never broke my heart.”
Joshua Thorn Canto VIII
Killed by Brannagan during the original Commission in Lunacy. His ghost presents a “flaming bill of particulars” when the Commission is reconvened. Brannagan’s murder of Joshua led to his deposition and marooning.
Juda O’Grogan-Brannagan Canto VI
Third-generation ruler. “A gentle man.” Married Rose Lunaria. His haunt comes to the death masks and sings “Rosa, Rosa, Rosa” softly, or strikes the bell in his watchtower.
K
Karl Sayon Epigraph
Author of “Come Bend Your Mind with Me,” the treatise quoted as the epigraph, which explains the Doomsday Equation and the Horseshoe Nail.
Kate Blithespirit Cantos III, VI, XII
“The Amazon.” From Camiroi. She wants to peek at the slave sale: “When there’s a circus in town, it sure is hard not to have at least a glimpse of the circus parade.” She represents Camiroi in the planet-destruction proposal.
Kaye Spencer Canto X
Proprietor of the Hay Meadow, the best hospitality house near the castle. Its desk captain denies that it is a hotel, calling it one of the houses opened “to our friends out of the hospitality of our hearts.”
Klepsis Throughout
The Thief Planet, the Pirate Planet. One of the three Trader Planets orbiting Beta Sun. “Klepsis has no history.” Governed as a Covenanted Piracy for two hundred years. Freshwater ocean, Ninety-Nine Continents, hallucinatory grapes, and painted feet. Clocks have not caught on, and visitors are told that the planet has no towns or geographical maps. The third focus of the Doomsday Equation is on its surface. Revealed in Canto XIII to share a planetary body with Tarshish.
L
Long John Tong Tyrone Throughout
The narrator. “Probably Eurasian” peg-leg, missing his left leg, historian, former substitute schoolteacher. Dubbed “Duke Tyrone the Historian” by Brannagan’s Ghost and entrusted with writing the Annals of Klepsis. Marries Princess Tharrala Thorn in Canto IV.
M
Malabu Worldwinger Cantos XI–XIII
“Exuberant young billionaire from World Abounding.” He booms “I can move anything.” He wants an epitaph better than Copernicus’s: “He could have moved them both.” Angela signs permits for him to move Tarshish. He requires four hundred shiploads of heavy equipment.
Margaret Brannagan Canto VI
Daughter of Christopher Brannagan, though her paternity is disputed and may belong to John Summers. Married Januarius O’Grogan. Mother of the ten pirate sons and their pirate sister Dina. Her testimony: “Joseph was my son only by adoption.”
Mechanical dogs Canto VI
Guardian animals of the treasure caverns. Armed with homing rockets, incinerating ray attack nodules, Beauclaire Blasters, and instant inactivating nerve gas. “Swiveled three hundred sixty degrees.” Controllable by the tax collector and by members of the Brannagan family.
Mock-Madeira Wine Canto VI
“Lively and sassy in its old nine-quart bottles.” Among the surviving provisions in the treasure caverns.
“My God What Grapes!” Cantos I, VII
The hallucinatory grapes of Klepsis, offered at customs. They are luscious beyond description and cause hallucination. The name becomes a proper-noun phrase: the “‘My God What Grapes!’ grapes.” Imported to Camiroi by young Klepsis Club enthusiasts.
N
“Nescio Unde” Canto X
Latin: “I don’t know from where.” The attribution of the expanded “For want of a nail” poem. Lafferty’s joke on untraceable sources.
Ninety-Nine Continents Canto II
The land masses of Klepsis: “really tentacled islands, of long extent down their peninsulas, but not of great area.” They make the ocean seem narrower than it is.
Ninety-percent rule Canto VII
Klepsis’s pragmatic test for reality: “if ninety percent of the people see a thing, then that thing is to be regarded as real.” Brannagan cannot remember whether this is called the “Pragmatic Sanction” or the “Categorical Imperative.”
O
O’Grogan’s Mountain Cantos X, XII, XIII
A low mountaintop near the castle. The company gathers here during the Doomsday crisis. Earthquakes split it while they stand on it. The slave-mathematician runs up its buckling slope with the amended equation.
Oliver Roundhead Cantos X, XIII
From Astrobe. At the Doomsday symposium (Canto X). In Canto XIII he takes a fast spacecraft to the Tarshish side of the planet and convinces Malabu Worldwinger to stop moving the world, an action that helps calm the tidal wave.
P
Parley birds Cantos II, X
Green-and-orange birds perched on people’s shoulders, like Gaea pirates’ parrots. They can be sent to repeat oral messages. A “wing-weary parley bird” tells Angela that Franco has bought the ship.
Particular Universe Epigraph, Canto I
The humanly inhabited universe: a kinetic three-dimensional ellipse with three foci, four suns, and seventeen habitable worlds, from Gaea-Earth to the Trader Planets. The Doomsday Equation treats it as unstable because its third focus approaches extinction.
Princess Placidia Canto VI
Daughter of Cloud Ravel-Brannagan and Brigid Hearn. Married Tarquin Thorn, who was murdered by a brother-in-law. Mother of Tharrala Thorn.
Q
Quasimodo Cantos II, IX, XI, XIII
A humpbacked dwarf, more than two hundred years old, identified as the Horseshoe Nail and the third focus of the Particular Universe. Kept asleep by law in the Sleeper’s Room of Ravel-Brannagan Castle, he is blind and gravely weakened. He claims that his mind contains all persons and things and that he is not alone in this: “There are two other persons in the universe whose minds also contain all things.” His approaching death triggers the final Doomsday crisis and the tolling of the En-Arche Bell.
R
Ravel-Brannagan Castle Cantos I–XIII
The seat of power on Klepsis. Six watchtowers named for six rulers. Contains the Whispering Room, the Sleeper’s Room, guest rooms with money-fonts, death masks on the walls, and passages to the treasure caverns. Brannagan’s walk-in tomb stands nearby and also connects to the caverns. Built of stone and brick with almost no wood.
Rose Lunaria Cantos IV, VI, XI
Wife of Juda O’Grogan-Brannagan. She appointed the tax collector in the treasure caverns. The mask through which Thorn used to look as a girl belongs to Rose. Juda’s ghost sings “Rosa, Rosa, Rosa.”
S
Saint Klingensmith, Order of Cantos VII, XI
The religious order to which the Green Robe belongs. He is “a thrice grandnephew” of Brannagan and serves as confessor and cigar-lighter.
Sebastian Jamaica Cantos VI, XII
A Klepsicle (native of Klepsis) among the company. Survives the massacre. Seated at the friends’ table in Canto XII.
Seneschal Fidelis Cantos VI–VII
“The soggy man.” Whipped and stabbed by Cloud Ravel’s servants and thought dead, then thrown into healing wine in derision. After fifty years in the wine, he emerges healed. Attends Brannagan’s Ghost in the walk-in tomb.
Sidonia Sopher Cantos X, XII
From Tarshish. She cannot give Tarshish’s astronomical data: “the data of Tarshish are not like the data of other worlds. They aren’t subject to detailed description.” Passengers on flights from Tarshish are blindfolded, deafened, and brain-pierced. She understands the mathematics but not in the Tarshish context.
Sleeper’s Room Canto XI
The room in Ravel-Brannagan Castle where Quasimodo lies on an old heaped-up bed. No windows, one chimney, believed to have only one door. Lined with death masks, some of which conceal torture traps. “One of the most haunted” rooms in the castle.
Sparaticus (green-eyed) Cantos III–XII
The enslaved brother of the blue-eyed crewman Sparaticus. At the slave sale, the blue-eyed brother buys his freedom after bidding against a gruff man. The blue-eyed Sparaticus is later killed by Prince Henry’s men; the green-eyed brother survives and joins the company.
“Sulphur-and-secret” Canto II
The additive used in the magnesium-steel from which Brannagan built his ocean ships. The secret component is never identified.
T
Tarshish Cantos V, VIII, X, XII, XIII
A supposedly separate, hidden world ruled by an anti-scientific clique. According to the Tarshish storyteller, its people are “unconscious and unborn” but build better spaceships than any conscious species. Passengers on flights from Tarshish are blindfolded, deafened, and brain-pierced. Revealed in Canto XIII to be the other half of the same planetary body as Klepsis.
Tarshish Storyteller Cantos V–VI, XIII
A blind, powerful storyteller who performs in the theatre-in-the-whale. His tales include tumbleweed-thistles, the unborn universe, and Bluebeard. He claims to have repeatedly dreamed the Doomsday crisis up to the point where the slave-mathematician is struck by lightning. “All good storytellers are blind.”
Tarshish Gin Slings Canto II
Beverages consumed by Brannagan’s Ghost. Prince Franco describes the practice in Canto II: “I give him Tarshish Gin Slings to drink, and he drinks them: and they are drunk. Can a ghost consume things of substance?”
Tarquin Thorn Canto VI
Married Princess Placidia. “Had some idea of being the second Elegant Interloper in the Brannagan line.” Failed in this and was murdered by a brother-in-law. Father of Tharrala Thorn.
Terpsichore Callagy Cantos I, IV, VIII, XIII
A two-legged visitor “into art.” She comes to study Klepsis’s deliberately bad art. She finds art treasures in the caverns. In the final canto she delivers the aesthetic verdict: “It’s consummate art whether we personally live or die.”
Tharrala Thorn Cantos III–XIII
Princess Tharrala Thorn, daughter of Placidia and Tarquin Thorn. Driven into the wilderness for “the unspeakable sin.” Acquired by Long John at the slave sale. She knows the castle’s secrets, commands the mechanical dogs, and studied under Flobert Traxley.
The Dina O’Grogan Cantos I–II, XII
A ship that “still sails that ocean.” Named for Dina O’Grogan-Brannagan, the pirate daughter. Prince Franco buys it in Canto I and the company sails to the castle.
Titus the Historian Cantos IX–XIII
Titus Livius Morrison-Bryce, another historian on Klepsis, who appears after Long John. He asks “Was there a coup last night?” and reports events during the Doomsday crisis.
U
“Unspeakable sin” Cantos IV, VI–VII, XI, XIII
The sin for which Tharrala Thorn was driven into the wilderness. Its nature is never revealed despite constant speculation: “What sin could possibly be unspeakable?” and “What evil could be beyond evil?” In the final canto, Thorn tells Angela she’ll reveal it “the first time I meet you after we’re both safely dead.”
W
Whispering Room Canto III
The strong room in Ravel-Brannagan Castle where the company is locked up. Equipped with one-way amplifiers: they can hear the entire castle, but no sound from the room reaches outside except to Brannagan’s Ghost.
Whittlesby Phenomenon Canto III
The phenomenon whereby one can hear the whistling of a falling man “although he is falling much faster than sound.” Mentioned during the insurance-advertisement stunt.
World Abounding Cantos II, XI–XIII
One of the seventeen worlds. Brannagan imported plants from here to Klepsis. Some of its animals can “go vague.” Malabu Worldwinger and the giant donkeys come from World Abounding.
